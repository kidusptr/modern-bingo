# Introduction

This document describes the complete emotional and gameplay journey of a player.

Rather than focusing on implementation, it focuses on the player's experience from the moment they launch the game until they decide to play again.

Every feature introduced into the game should improve one or more stages of this journey.

If a feature cannot be mapped to this document, its purpose should be questioned.

# Stage 1 — Launch Game

## Purpose

Create an immediate feeling of excitement.

The player should feel welcomed back into a living world.

---

## Player Goals

- Open the game.
- See what's new.
- Continue progression.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Curiosity**

Questions the player should naturally have:

- What's happening today?
- Did I unlock anything?
- Are my friends online?
- What should I do next?

---

# Stage 2 — Home Screen

## Purpose

Provide a clear overview of the player's world.

The player should immediately understand:

- Their progression.
- Current events.
- Available rewards.
- Friends online.
- Primary action.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Belonging**

The home screen should feel alive rather than static.

---

# Stage 3 — Daily Progress

Players collect rewards, complete missions, and review progression.

Nothing here should feel like mandatory chores.

Instead, these systems should reinforce that every day offers something new.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Motivation**

---

# Stage 4 — Choosing a Game Mode

Players decide how they want to play.

This should feel like choosing an experience rather than selecting a menu option.

Every mode should communicate its personality before the player enters.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Anticipation**

---

# Stage 5 — Matchmaking

Players wait to enter a match.

Waiting should never feel like waiting.

The game may use this time to display:

- Tips
- Friends joining
- Match statistics
- Fun facts
- Missions
- Cosmetics
- Player introductions

---

## Desired Emotion

**Expectation**

---

# Stage 6 — Lobby

The lobby is where strangers become competitors.

Players begin seeing:

- Opponents
- Friends
- Reactions
- Avatars
- Card themes
- Arena

This is the final moment before competition begins.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Energy**

Players should think:

> "Let's do this."

---

# Stage 7 — Card Reveal

Receiving a card is a surprisingly emotional moment.

It becomes:

"My card."

Not simply:

"A card."

Animations and presentation should create ownership.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Ownership**

---

# Stage 8 — Countdown

The countdown builds anticipation.

Players mentally prepare.

The match is about to begin.

Nothing should interrupt this rhythm.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Excitement**

---

# Stage 9 — Early Game

The first numbers appear.

Players begin marking their cards.

Everyone remains optimistic.

No one is far behind.

Hope is at its highest.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Hope**

---

# Stage 10 — Mid Game

The match begins taking shape.

Different players progress at different speeds.

Near misses begin appearing.

Social reactions become more frequent.

Competition naturally develops.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Focus**

---

# Stage 11 — Critical Stage

Multiple players approach victory.

The pace naturally feels faster.

Every number matters.

Players stop casually watching.

Everyone pays attention.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Suspense**

---

# Stage 12 — Heartbeat Mode

When appropriate, the game enters its most dramatic state.

Everything slows down.

Animations become more cinematic.

Audio changes.

Attention narrows.

Every draw could end the match.

Heartbeat Mode represents the emotional climax of the experience.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Adrenaline**

---

# Stage 13 — Winner Announcement

The winner deserves celebration.

The victory presentation should feel meaningful.

Other players should enjoy witnessing it rather than immediately leaving.

Winning should create admiration instead of interruption.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Celebration**

---

# Stage 14 — Rewards

Regardless of the outcome, players receive meaningful progression.

Rewards should reinforce that every match mattered.

Examples include:

- Experience
- Coins
- Missions
- Achievements
- Statistics
- Seasonal progress

---

## Desired Emotion

**Satisfaction**

---

# Stage 15 — Post Match Summary

Players reflect on the match.

Interesting moments may be highlighted.

Examples:

- Closest Finish
- Most Numbers Marked
- Longest Streak
- Fastest Mark
- MVP Moments

Players should relive memorable moments before leaving.

---

## Desired Emotion

**Reflection**

---

# Stage 16 — Play Again

The final screen has one purpose.

Encourage another match.

Not through pressure.

Through excitement.

Players should naturally think:

> "One more."

---

## Desired Emotion

**Anticipation**

# Success Criteria

A successful player journey should make players feel that:

- Every match is worth playing.
- Every session contributes to long-term progress.
- Winning feels exciting.
- Losing still feels rewarding.
- Friends make the experience better.
- Every stage naturally leads to the next.
- The game respects their time.
- The game creates memorable stories.

---

# Closing Statement

The player journey is the backbone of the entire game.

Every mechanic, feature, animation, sound, and system should strengthen one or more stages of this journey.

When making design decisions, we should ask a simple question:

> **Does this improve the player's journey?**

If the answer is yes, it belongs.

If not, it should be reconsidered.
